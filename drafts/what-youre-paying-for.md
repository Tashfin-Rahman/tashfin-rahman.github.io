# What you are actually paying for in a fit-out quote

---

Most people ask for a price before anyone has seen the room. That is fair
enough. You want to know whether this is affordable before you spend an
afternoon on it. But a number given at that stage is a guess, and a guess has
to be padded to be safe, so you end up hearing a figure larger than the work
would actually cost.

Here is how a quote gets built where I work, and what moves it.

## It starts with the room, not a price list

We visit, measure, and ask what the space is for. This matters more than it
sounds. A wardrobe built for a room with a low ceiling is a different piece of
work from the same wardrobe with another foot of height, even though both are
"a wardrobe" on a price list. One needs a different carcass, different hinges,
and a different way of getting it through the door.

Catalogue furniture solves this by making you fit the room to the product.
Made-to-order solves it the other way round, which is why it has to be costed
before it can be priced.

## What goes into the number

Every job breaks down into roughly the same parts:

- **Materials.** Board, timber, laminate, hardware, glass, paint. This is the
  part clients expect, and it is rarely the part that moves a quote.
- **Workshop labour.** Cutting, assembly, finishing. We build in our own
  workshop rather than subcontracting, so this is a cost I can see and control
  rather than a margin someone else is taking.
- **Site work.** Delivery, carrying, installation, and making good. A third
  floor with no lift is a different job from a ground floor with one.

## Where quotes go wrong

Three things move a number after it has been agreed, and all three are
avoidable.

**Scope creep after work starts.** A client adds two drawers, then asks for a
different handle, then wants the unit a little wider. Each change is small.
Together they are not. We price changes as they come rather than at the end,
so nobody is surprised at handover.

**Site surprises.** A wall that is not square, a floor that slopes, a beam

## What a client can do

Decide the finish early. Finish choices move a quote more than dimensions do,
and changing your mind about a laminate after the carcass is cut means paying
for both.

Ask for the breakdown. Any firm that cannot show you where the money goes is
either guessing or hiding something. We write it out because it is easier to
defend a number you can see.

Let the measurement happen before you ask for the price. It costs you an
afternoon and it saves you the padding.
