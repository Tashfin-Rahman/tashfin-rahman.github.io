What moves a fit-out quote

October 2026 · Tashfin Rahman, Home Inn

Clients usually assume the materials decide the price. In my experience materials are the part people expect, and rarely the part that moves a quote after it has been agreed. Change the five decisions below and watch which ones actually do.

This is a model, not a price list. The number it gives is an index: the quote you started from is 100, and everything after that is movement away from it. The shares between the five bands are illustrative, chosen to show which decisions matter and which barely do. They are not Home Inn’s costing, and no amount of money is implied. If you want a price, the first step is letting somebody measure the room.

    What the model is saying

    Two of these five are decisions a client makes freely, and they are the two that move the number most. The finish is chosen before anything is cut, so deciding it late, or changing it afterwards, means paying for the work twice. Changes after cutting starts are each small on their own, which is exactly why they add up without anyone noticing.

    The other three are facts about the job rather than choices. A room that is out of true, a third floor with no lift, joinery that has to be drawn from scratch: none of those can be negotiated away, and all of them should be in the number before anyone signs rather than discovered later.
