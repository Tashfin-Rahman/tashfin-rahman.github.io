# Writing a post

You write it. I publish it. That is the split you picked.

## What to do

Put the text in a file in this folder, named after what it is about. A `.md`
or a `.txt` file is fine, and so is plain text pasted into the chat. For
example `drafts/quoting-by-phone.md`.

It does not need to be tidy. Headings as plain lines, no formatting, half a
sentence left unfinished: all fine. What matters is that the thinking is
yours.

Then tell me it is there. I will turn it into a page on the site, write the
bits search engines and AI assistants read, add it to the blog index, the
sitemap and the feed, and push it live. You never touch HTML.

## What helps

**One real example beats three general points.** Both pieces on the site argue
from method, which works, but a piece with one job in it would be worth more
than either. No client has to be named.

**A number you actually know.** Not an estimate for the sake of having one. If
you do not have one, say so and I will write around it rather than inventing
something. That rule does not change.

**Say what you got wrong.** The bookkeeping piece works because it names a
habit you had to drop. That is the part people remember.

## Length

Anything from 400 to 1,200 words. The two live pieces are around 800. Shorter
is fine if the point is made.

## Four questions still open

These came out of the fit-out piece and were written around rather than
answered. Any one of them would sharpen a future post, and none needs a
client's permission:

1. Roughly what proportion of sheet material ends up as offcut on a typical
   job?
2. Is there a standard contingency percentage, or is it judged per job?
3. A real site surprise you have hit. A wall out of square, a floor that
   slopes, a beam nobody mentioned. What did it cost to solve?
4. How long does Home Inn hold a quote for before the price moves?

Answers to 1 and 2 would also let me replace the illustrative shares in
`/tools/fit-out-quote/` with your real ones, which would make that page a
great deal stronger than it is.

## What is in this folder

Nothing here is published. `_config.yml` keeps the whole folder out of the
built site, so a half-finished draft cannot go live by accident.

- `work-brand-identity.js` — the Home Inn brand identity entry, ready to put
  back into Selected Work whenever you want it.
- `work-entries.js` — four older entries, superseded.
- `images/` — images that were being served from the site with nothing
  pointing at them.
